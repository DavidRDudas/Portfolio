The library

    
Every book that can be written is on these shelves: 410 pages, 40 lines to a page, 80 characters to a line, from the 95 characters a keyboard can type. That makes 951,312,000 books, a number 2,594,774 digits long. Almost all of them are noise.

    
One of them lets you out. Its place was worked out from its text, the same way every book's is, and it's on your side of the chasm. Put it through the slot at any rest area.

    
How hard is it to find? Put an electron at one edge of the observable universe and move it the width of a proton. Wait for the universe to reach heat death, about 10100 years, then move it again. When it gets to the other edge, start on the next electron, until all 1080 have crossed. If you read a book every second for all that time, you'd get through about 10229 books. That's 0.000…0007% of the library, with another 2,594,536 zeros where the dots are.

    

      
      
    

    
After Steven L. Peck's A Short Stay in Hell and Jorge Luis Borges's The Library of Babel.
